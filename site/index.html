What's the difference between a bank loan and a broker loan?

A bank sells you its own products at retail. A mortgage broker works the wholesale channel — I compare pricing across many lenders and bring you the best fit.

I like to compare it to shopping at Costco instead of a corner supermarket. Wholesale moves far more volume, so the pricing is usually better, and if one lender's guidelines don't fit your situation, I just move the file to one whose guidelines do. A bank can't do that.

What is PMI, and how do I get rid of it?

PMI is private mortgage insurance. It applies when you put down less than 20%, and it protects the lender — not you — if the loan defaults.

Once you've built 20% equity you can request that it be removed. There are also structures that avoid it entirely, like lender-paid mortgage insurance or a piggyback second. Which one wins depends on how long you plan to hold the loan, and that's a five-minute conversation.

Can I get a mortgage if I'm self-employed?

Absolutely — a large share of my clients are. Most lenders will want two years of tax returns to document income, and a current profit-and-loss statement can support it.

If your returns show a lot of write-offs, conventional guidelines may not tell your real story. That's where bank statement and asset-depletion programs come in. Bring me the returns and I'll tell you honestly which door is the right one.

I co-signed for a family member. Does that hurt me?

It can, but it's fixable. To exclude that other property's payment from your debt ratio, we document 12 months of payments made by the other party — canceled checks or bank statements showing the money left their account.

Without that documentation, you have to qualify carrying both housing payments. This comes up constantly in multigenerational households, so start gathering the statements early.

Are there hidden fees?

No. Every fee is disclosed up front, in writing, on your Loan Estimate — that's federal law, and it's also just how I prefer to work.

If you choose a lender-credit ("no-cost") structure, the non-recurring closing costs are credited back to you. Pre-paid items — interest between loans, property taxes, insurance and HOA dues — are separate and aren't included in that.

Is there a prepayment penalty?

On conventional, FHA and VA loans there's no prepayment penalty — pay extra, pay it off, or refinance whenever it suits you.

Two caveats. As a broker I ask that you keep the loan 180 days before refinancing again. And some Non-QM programs, the ones that qualify you without verifying income, may carry a penalty — you'll hear that from me up front, not at signing.
