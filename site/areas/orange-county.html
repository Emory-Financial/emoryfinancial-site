Mello-Roos will change what you can afford — plan for it

This is the Orange County issue nobody warns buyers about. Irvine, Ladera Ranch, Rancho Mission Viejo and most newer master-planned communities carry Mello-Roos (Community Facilities District) assessments on top of the base property tax. It can add hundreds of dollars a month, it counts fully against your debt-to-income ratio, and it can take an approved buyer out of qualification. Get the actual assessment for the specific parcel before you fall in love with the house — not the community average.

HOA dues stack on top of that

Master-planned communities frequently have both a master association and a sub-association. Two sets of dues, plus Mello-Roos, plus base taxes. I build all four into the qualification from the start so your pre-approval reflects what you can genuinely carry.

New construction has its own timeline

Builder financing incentives are real but they're not always the better deal, and builder lock periods on a home completing in eight months work very differently from a resale lock. Worth comparing the builder's lender against the wholesale market before you sign the incentive addendum.

Orange County sits at the high-balance ceiling

The 2026 conforming limit here is $1,249,125 for a single-family home — the highest tier federal law allows. That covers a lot of Orange County, which means conforming pricing on purchases people assume must be jumbo.
