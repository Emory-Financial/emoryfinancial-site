Worried About AI and Your Job? Here's What It Means for Your Mortgage

By Derek Shao, NMLS 242117 · October 16, 2026 · 4 min read

The short answer: AI hasn't caused widespread layoffs yet, but it is changing hiring, especially for early-career office jobs. If you own a home, the smartest moves happen while your paycheck is steady: build cash reserves, open a home equity line before you need it, and refinance or buy while your income is easy to document. Lenders approve the job you have today, not the one you'll have next year.

What's actually happening with AI and jobs?

The honest picture in 2026 is more mixed than the headlines. Overall hiring has held up, and job openings were near a two-year high this spring. The pressure is concentrated at the bottom of the ladder. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab found that employment for 22- to 25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations, such as software and customer service, fell by double digits after late 2022, while older workers in the same jobs held steady (Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, 2025).

In plain terms: AI is less likely to take a seasoned professional's job tomorrow than to change what the job looks like, slow raises in some fields, and make the next job search take longer. For a household with a mortgage, that last part is the one to plan for.

How does job uncertainty affect my existing mortgage?

It doesn't, directly. Your rate, payment, and terms are locked in by your note. No lender re-checks your employment after closing. What changes is your flexibility:

  • Refinancing requires current, verifiable income. If you're between jobs, you generally can't refinance until you're re-employed and have pay stubs.
  • Home equity lines require the same. Banks often freeze or lower HELOC limits in downturns, but a line that's already open is far easier to keep than a new one is to get.
  • Buying the next home gets harder during a job change. Most lenders want a stable two-year history, and a new field or a new 1099 arrangement can mean waiting.

What should I do now, while my income is steady?

  1. Know your cushion. Aim for six months of total housing cost (mortgage, taxes, insurance, HOA) in savings. In Los Angeles County that's often $30,000 to $50,000.
  2. Open a HELOC you don't plan to use. It's a backup emergency fund. Many have no or low annual fees. Draw nothing unless you need it.
  3. Refinance now if the math works. If today's rates beat yours, or you want to remove mortgage insurance, do it while your employer letter and W-2s are clean.
  4. Buy on your current income, not your hoped-for raise. Size the payment so it still works if a bonus or commission disappears.
  5. If you're thinking of going independent, talk to me before you quit. A W-2 to 1099 switch can pause your borrowing options for up to two years on conventional loans. Bank-statement programs can help, but planning ahead is better.

What if I'm laid off and have a mortgage?

Call your servicer before you miss a payment, not after. Ask about forbearance and loss-mitigation options; they exist for exactly this. Severance can count as reserves but usually not as qualifying income. Don't drain retirement accounts to make the payment until you've spoken with your servicer and a tax professional. And call me: sometimes the best move is a plan for the next 12 months, not a new loan today.

Is AI changing how mortgages are underwritten?

Yes, mostly behind the scenes. Automated underwriting has existed for decades; AI is now speeding up document review and income calculation. That helps clean files close faster. It doesn't replace judgment on complicated files: self-employed income, multiple properties, family gifts. That's where a broker who can move your file to a different lender still earns his keep.

They were the ones who arranged their options while things were calm. If you'd like a 30-minute review of where you stand, no credit pull, I'm happy to do it.

Frequently asked questions

Can my lender call my loan if I lose my job?

No. As long as payments are made, employment status after closing doesn't affect your loan.

Will a HELOC hurt my credit if I don't use it?

Opening one adds a hard inquiry and a new account, usually a small, temporary dip. An unused line can help your utilization ratio.

Can I get a mortgage on severance pay?

Severance usually counts as an asset, not income. You'll generally need a new job with an offer letter or pay stubs.

Should I pay my mortgage down faster in case of a layoff?

Usually not first. Cash in the bank helps in a layoff; extra principal paid into the house doesn't lower your monthly payment.
