A Historic El Niño Is Building, and Your Homeowners Policy Doesn't Cover Floods. What California Owners and Buyers Should Do Before December

By Derek Shao, NMLS 242117 · November 17, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer: NOAA expects a strong-to-very strong El Niño to last through early 2027 and favors a wetter-than-average winter from California to Florida. Most California homeowners and renters policies don't cover flooding or mudslides, and a flood policy from the National Flood Insurance Program usually takes 30 days to start. Your lender only requires flood insurance if your home is in a high-risk zone, so most California homeowners don't have it. If you want coverage before the heaviest storms arrive, now is the time to look. If you're buying, coverage bought at closing can start the day you close.

As of November 17, 2026. Forecast odds are from NOAA's Climate Prediction Center and can change month to month.

How strong is this El Niño expected to be?

Very strong, by the government's own forecasts. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center has an El Niño Advisory in effect. In its November 12 update, it said a strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January–March 2027, with a greater than 79% chance. It put the odds of a "historic" event, stronger than any El Niño since 1950, at about 60% for October–December and November–January (CPC ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, November 12, 2026, as posted by Coyote Gulch; CPC ENSO page).

NOAA's winter outlook, released October 15, favors wetter-than-average conditions across the southern U.S., "from California to Florida." National Weather Service Director Ken Graham said "a very strong El Nino will likely bring a southward and eastward shift to the jet stream," and warned that heavy rain on dry ground can cause flash floods and mudslides (NOAA, October 15, 2026). Governor Newsom declared a statewide state of emergency on September 21 to prepare for the "developing 2026–2027 El Niño event" (Proclamation, September 21, 2026).

None of this guarantees your street floods. Forecasters say the odds tilt wetter, and the Climate Prediction Center notes that with an event this big, the chances of El Niño-like impacts are "larger, though not guaranteed."

Does my homeowners insurance cover flooding?

Usually not. The California Department of Insurance says homeowners, renters and commercial policies typically exclude flooding, mudslides and debris flows. There's an important exception: when a recent wildfire or another covered peril causes the mudslide or debris flow. The Commissioner has reminded insurers of their legal duty to cover that damage in burn areas. Some water damage from wind-driven rain may be covered, and damage from a fallen tree generally is. Renters: your landlord's policy doesn't cover your belongings (CDI, September 22, 2026).

Few Californians have separate flood coverage. Only 1.4% of California residential properties carry flood insurance, according to a Neptune Flood analysis of FEMA data reported by Insurance Business (Insurance Business, November 16, 2026).

Does my mortgage lender require flood insurance?

Only if your home is in a high-risk flood zone. FEMA marks high-risk areas with zones starting with A or V. If you have a federally backed mortgage on a home there, flood insurance is a condition of the loan. Zones B, C and X are moderate- to low-risk, so the lender doesn't require it. But FEMA says the risk there is "reduced, but not completely removed," and "one in three insurance claims come from moderate- to low-risk flood areas" (FloodSmart, What does my flood zone mean?).

For loans sold to Fannie Mae in a high-risk zone, the required coverage is the lesser of the replacement cost of the home, the maximum NFIP coverage available, or the loan balance (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B7-3-06). Since 2016, most regulated lenders must also escrow flood premiums on residential loans in those zones. HELOCs, second liens and condos covered by an association's master flood policy are among the exceptions (Federal Reserve, Consumer Compliance Outlook).

To see your zone, search your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.

Why does the 30-day waiting period matter right now?

A flood policy you buy on your own generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, which is why CDI says to buy before a storm approaches. NFIP covers up to $250,000 for a home's structure and up to $100,000 for belongings (FloodSmart agent guide, coverage). Private flood policies are also sold in California. Insurance press reports their waiting periods typically run 1 to 15 days, depending on the insurer (Live Insurance News, November 16, 2026).

The wildfire exception is narrower than many people assume. The fire has to be on federal land, so check with your agent before counting on it.

I'm buying a home this winter. What should I do?

Ask for the flood zone early. Your lender orders a flood determination as part of the loan, but you can look up the address yourself the day you're in contract. If the home is in an A or V zone, get a flood quote during your contingency period, not the week of closing. The premium becomes part of your monthly housing cost, which is what lenders look at when they figure what you qualify for, and in those zones it's usually collected through escrow. If the home is outside the high-risk zone, consider coverage anyway. Buying it with your loan is one of the few ways to skip the 30-day wait.

I wrote about what to ask on the homeowners side in Shopping for home insurance as a California buyer this fall.

What if a storm damages my home?

Call your insurer and your mortgage servicer. Your lender may hold the dwelling portion of a large claim and release it as repairs progress, and California law (AB 493) requires lenders to pay at least 2% simple interest a year on insurance money they hold while you repair. I walked through how that works in what your mortgage company owes you on insurance payouts. If a disaster makes it hard to keep up with payments, call your servicer before you miss one and ask about options.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding in California?

Generally no. The California Department of Insurance says homeowners and renters policies typically exclude flooding, mudslides and debris flows, except when a recent wildfire or other covered peril causes the damage.

How long does flood insurance take to start?

An NFIP policy bought on its own usually takes effect 30 days after purchase. If you buy it in connection with a mortgage closing, it can start at closing. Private flood policies set their own waiting periods.

Is flood insurance required for my mortgage?

Federal rules require it when the home is in a FEMA high-risk zone (zones beginning with A or V) and the mortgage is federally backed. Outside those zones it's usually optional, but FEMA says one in three flood claims come from moderate- and low-risk areas.

How much does NFIP flood insurance cover?

Up to $250,000 for a home's structure and up to $100,000 for belongings.

How do I find my flood zone?

Search your address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center at msc.fema.gov.
