Only 19% of Californians Can Afford a Median-Priced Home. Here's the Income It Takes in Your County

By Derek Shao, NMLS 242117 · November 3, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer: In the third quarter of 2026, only 19% of California households earned enough to buy the statewide median-priced home of $894,630, according to the California Association of Realtors. The minimum qualifying income was $228,000 statewide and $244,000 in Los Angeles County. Lower prices offset higher rates over the summer, but the 30-year national average has stayed above the rate C.A.R. assumed, so the bar today is likely a bit higher than the report shows. Condos, inland counties, more income on the application and lower monthly debts are how most buyers I work with are closing the gap.

As of November 3, 2026. Figures are C.A.R.'s quarterly index and Freddie Mac's national survey average, not quotes or offers of credit.

How affordable is a California home right now?

Not very, and it hasn't moved in a year. The California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) reported on October 30 that 19% of households could afford the median-priced existing single-family home in the third quarter of 2026, unchanged from the second quarter and from a year earlier. The statewide median price was $894,630, and C.A.R. estimated a household needed at least $228,000 a year to qualify. Nationally, 39% of households could afford a median-priced home of $432,900, with a minimum income of $110,400 (C.A.R., October 30, 2026).

The reason affordability held steady is a tug-of-war. The statewide median fell 2.3% from the second quarter, according to coverage of the same report, while C.A.R.'s effective 30-year rate rose to 6.80% from 6.54% (Mortgage Professional America, November 2, 2026). Lower prices canceled out higher borrowing costs.

Condos and townhomes were the one bright spot: 31% of households could afford the $640,000 median, up from 30%, with a minimum qualifying income of $163,200.

In Los Angeles County, the median was $958,450 and 15% of households could afford it. The broader Los Angeles metro area, which includes more moderately priced neighborhoods, came in at 18% with a $215,600 minimum income. In the Inland Empire it was 26% and $152,800.

Is it harder to qualify today than the report shows?

Probably a little. C.A.R.'s figures reflect the summer quarter, at an effective rate of 6.80%. Freddie Mac's national 30-year average was 7.24% for the week of October 29, 2026, its lowest reading of the month but still well above the 6.80% C.A.R. used, and up from 6.17% a year earlier (Freddie Mac PMMS). When rates are higher, the same price takes more income to qualify, so if prices hold, fourth-quarter numbers could look tighter.

Freddie Mac figures are national survey averages for borrowers with strong credit and 20% down, not rates offered by Emory Financial.

Does the "minimum income" number mean I can't buy?

No. It's a useful yardstick, not the rule a lender uses. C.A.R.'s index tests one household buying the median-priced home with a standard set of assumptions. A real approval looks at your actual numbers, mainly your debt-to-income ratio: your total monthly debts (including the new housing payment) divided by your gross monthly income.

On conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae, the maximum DTI is 36% for manually underwritten loans, up to 45% with strong credit and reserves, and up to 50% for loans approved through Fannie Mae's automated system, Desktop Underwriter (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-6-02). That doesn't mean stretching to 50% is a good idea. It does mean a buyer with no car payment or student loans can sometimes qualify for more than a headline figure suggests, and a buyer carrying a lot of monthly debt may qualify for less.

What are California buyers doing to close the gap?

These are the levers I see make the biggest difference, roughly in order of how often they come up:

A few notes on each:

  • Condos and townhomes. The affordability rate is 12 points higher than for single-family homes statewide. HOA dues count in your debt ratio, and California condo buyers should look closely at the association's reserves; I covered what the new reserve law means in California's HOA reserve law for condo owners and buyers.
  • Co-borrowers and family help. A parent, sibling or partner on the loan adds their income, but also their debts. A gift for the down payment lowers the loan amount without changing anyone's income. I walk through both in helping an adult child buy a home.
  • Variable income. If you earn bonuses, commissions or overtime, how a lender counts that income can move your number more than anything else on this list. See how lenders count bonus and commission income.
  • Debts. Paying off a small installment loan or a credit card balance before you apply can lower your DTI. Talk to your loan officer first so you don't drain cash you'll need for closing or reserves.

Reading from outside California? The Fannie Mae debt-ratio rules apply nationwide. The affordability and income figures above are California-specific.

Derek\'s take

Every quarter, the headline says only one in five Californians can afford a home, and every quarter I meet buyers who assume that means them. Sometimes it does, for now. But the index measures one household buying one median-priced house, and very few of my clients are exactly that household. Your real number depends on what you're buying, where, who's on the loan and what you already owe each month. Before you write off this market, or before you stretch for a house that would leave you house-poor, let's look at what you actually qualify for and what payment you'd be comfortable with. Frequently asked questions

What percentage of Californians can afford a home in 2026?

According to the California Association of Realtors, 19% of households could afford the median-priced existing single-family home in the third quarter of 2026, unchanged from the prior quarter and a year earlier. For condos and townhomes, it was 31%.

How much do you need to make to buy a house in California?

C.A.R. estimated a minimum qualifying income of $228,000 for the statewide median-priced home of $894,630 in Q3 2026. In Los Angeles County it was $244,000; in Riverside County, $161,200; in San Bernardino County, $131,200.

How much income do I need to buy a condo in California?

C.A.R. estimated $163,200 for the statewide median-priced condo or townhome of $640,000 in Q3 2026.

What debt-to-income ratio do I need for a conventional loan?

Under Fannie Mae's guidelines, the maximum is 36% for manually underwritten loans, up to 45% with strong credit and reserves, and up to 50% for loans approved through Desktop Underwriter. Individual lenders can set stricter limits.

Why didn't California affordability improve if home prices fell?

Prices dipped over the summer, but the effective mortgage rate in C.A.R.'s index rose from 6.54% to 6.80%, which offset the savings.
