Buying a Truck or Equipment Before December 31? What That Write-Off Means for Your 2027 Mortgage

By Derek Shao, NMLS 242117 · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer: If you're self-employed and plan to buy or refinance in 2027, a year-end truck or equipment purchase usually hurts your mortgage less through the tax write-off than through the cash and the payment. On a Fannie Mae loan, the lender adds depreciation, including a big first-year write-off, back to your income. What it doesn't add back is the cash that left your accounts, a new loan payment in your name, or supplies and expenses you prepay in December. Make the purchase decision with your tax preparer and your mortgage broker in the same conversation.

As of November 14, 2026. Tax figures come from the IRS and lending rules from the Fannie Mae Selling Guide pages cited below. Your tax preparer and lender apply them to your own situation.

Why does this come up every November?

Because December is when a lot of contractors, truckers and small-business owners decide whether to buy that truck, trailer or piece of equipment "for the write-off." The rules for 2026 are generous. For tax years beginning in 2026, the IRS says you can elect to expense up to $2,560,000 of qualifying property under Section 179, with that limit reduced once the property you place in service passes $4,090,000. The Section 179 amount for a sport utility vehicle is capped at $32,000 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Separately, the IRS says bonus depreciation is 100% for qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 (IRS, Working Families Tax Cuts business provisions).

The timing matters. Under Section 179 you deduct the cost "in the year you place the property in service," which the IRS defines as when it's "ready and available for a specific use" (IRS Publication 946). Equipment that arrives in January lands on your 2027 return, not your 2026 one.

None of that is a reason to buy or not buy. That's a tax and business decision. My job is to tell you what it looks like to a mortgage underwriter.

Does a big depreciation write-off lower the income a lender can use?

Usually not, on a conventional loan sold to Fannie Mae. If you file a Schedule C, Fannie Mae's guide lists depreciation, depletion, business use of a home, amortization and casualty losses as items to add back in the cash-flow analysis (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-3.6-03). The worksheet lenders use, Form 1084, adds back depreciation line by line. If you take the standard mileage rate on a vehicle, it adds back a depreciation amount based on your business miles (Fannie Mae Form 1084).

So the tax deduction from expensing a truck mostly gets reversed for qualifying purposes. Two caveats. First, the underwriter also has to look at year-to-year trends in your gross income, expenses and taxable income (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-3.2-01), so a sudden drop in taxable income can raise questions even when the math works. A short explanation letter helps. Second, other loan types and non-QM programs use their own methods. A bank-statement loan looks at deposits, not your return.

What does hurt?

The cash. Fannie Mae allows business assets for the down payment, closing costs and reserves, but before using them the lender "must perform a business cash flow analysis to confirm that the withdrawal of funds" for the home "will not have a negative impact on the business" (B3-3.2-01). If December's purchase empties the business account, that gets harder to show.

The payment. If the equipment or truck loan is in your personal name, it's your debt. Fannie Mae lets a lender leave a business-paid debt out of your ratio only when the account has no history of delinquency, the business shows the obligation was paid out of company funds (such as a year of canceled company checks), and the lender's cash-flow analysis already accounted for the payment (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-6-05). A loan you open in December won't have that year of history by spring.

Ordinary expenses. Stocking up on supplies, prepaying next year's costs or paying year-end bonuses all reduce taxable income, and they aren't on the add-back list. I walked through which deductions lenders add back in my earlier piece on self-employed tax returns.

Which tax return will my lender actually use?

It depends on when your loan closes. Fannie Mae defines the "most recent" return as the last one scheduled to have been filed with the IRS, and ties the requirement to the loan's dates (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B1-1-03). For someone buying in 2027:

A purchase you make before December 31 shows up on your 2026 return. If you're buying in the first few months of 2027, the lender is still mostly looking at 2025, but your bank statements will show the cash that left and your credit report will show any new loan. If you're buying later in 2027, the 2026 return becomes the main event.

Derek\'s take

I see this every year. Someone buys a truck in December because the accountant said it would save on taxes. Then in March, when they want to make an offer, the write-off isn't the problem. The problem is that the business account is lighter, there's a new payment on the credit report, and nobody looked at both at once. Sometimes the purchase is still the right call. It's just better to know the trade-off before you sign. Frequently asked questions

Does Section 179 or bonus depreciation reduce my mortgage qualifying income?

On a Fannie Mae conventional loan, generally no. Depreciation is on the list of items added back to Schedule C income in the lender's cash-flow analysis. Other loan programs may treat it differently, and a big drop in taxable income can still prompt questions about your business trend.

Will a new truck loan hurt my mortgage application?

It can. If the loan is in your name, the payment usually counts in your debt-to-income ratio. Fannie Mae lets a lender exclude it only when the business has documented paying it, typically with a year of company checks, there's no late history, and the payment is already reflected in the business cash-flow analysis.

Can I use money in my business account for a down payment?

Often yes. Fannie Mae accepts business assets for the down payment, closing costs and reserves, but the lender must confirm through a cash-flow analysis that taking the money out won't hurt the business.

If I buy equipment in December, which return will my lender see?

The purchase lands on your 2026 return if it's placed in service by December 31. A Fannie Mae loan closing before April 15, 2027 generally relies on your 2025 return, while loans closing later in 2027 increasingly rely on 2026.

Should I skip the purchase if I'm buying a home next year?

Not necessarily. It's a business and tax decision first. The point is to weigh the cash, any new payment and your timing alongside the tax savings before you commit.
