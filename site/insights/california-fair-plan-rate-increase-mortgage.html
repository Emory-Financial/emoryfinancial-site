The FAIR Plan's 29% Rate Increase Is Here: What It Means for Your Mortgage Payment

By Derek Shao, NMLS 242117 · October 18, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer: On October 15, 2026, California FAIR Plan premiums rose an average of 29.1% for new and renewing policies, the largest increase in the plan's recent history. If your insurance is paid through your mortgage escrow account, the higher premium will show up in your monthly payment after your servicer's next escrow review. Homes in high wildfire-risk areas will see the biggest jumps, some lower-risk homes will see decreases, and wildfire-mitigation discounts and a fresh look at the private market are the best ways to soften the hit.

As of October 18, 2026.

What exactly changed on October 15?

The FAIR Plan, California's insurer of last resort, asked the Department of Insurance in September 2025 for an average 35.8% increase on its homeowner (dwelling) policies. The Department approved an average of 29.1% (the Department's own summary rounds it to 29% versus 36% requested), effective October 15, 2026 for new and renewing policies (CDI, May 20, 2026; KQED, August 11, 2026).

"Average" is doing a lot of work in that number. According to reporting on the filing, the increase is concentrated in the wildfire portion of the premium: homes in fire-prone areas pay the most, some wildfire premiums will double, and some lower-risk urban policyholders will see decreases (KQED; Salinas Californian via Yahoo Finance, May 22, 2026). In parts of Los Angeles County like Malibu, and in mountain communities like Lake Arrowhead, about half of homes are insured through the FAIR Plan, KQED reported.

How will the FAIR Plan increase affect my monthly mortgage payment?

If you have an escrow (impound) account, your servicer pays your insurance and property taxes for you and collects the cost in your monthly payment. Federal rules (RESPA's Regulation X) require your servicer to review the account at least once a year and send you an annual statement (12 CFR 1024.17). When your renewal premium goes up, the next review will usually show two things:

  1. A higher monthly escrow amount going forward, to cover the new premium.
  2. A shortage, if the account paid a bigger bill than it collected for. If the shortage is one month's escrow payment or more, the servicer can leave it alone or spread repayment over at least 12 months; it cannot demand a lump sum. Smaller shortages can be billed within 30 days. You can always choose to pay a shortage early to keep the monthly amount lower.

The servicer can also hold a cushion of up to one-sixth of a year's escrow disbursements, so the cushion grows when the premium does.

A hypothetical, for scale: if a FAIR Plan premium of $3,000 a year rose by exactly the 29.1% average, it would become about $3,873, or roughly $73 more per month through escrow, before any shortage or cushion adjustment. Your actual premium change could be much higher or lower.

If you pay your insurance yourself (no escrow), you'll see the change directly on your renewal bill. Just make sure it's paid on time: if coverage lapses, your servicer can buy "force-placed" insurance for you, which is typically expensive and covers less.

Does my lender accept FAIR Plan coverage?

In my experience, yes, as long as the total coverage meets the investor's rules. For a Fannie Mae loan on a one-to-four-unit home, the property must be insured on a replacement-cost basis, the deductible can't exceed 5% of the coverage amount, and if one policy leaves out a required peril, the borrower has to add another policy to fill the gap (Fannie Mae Selling Guide B7-3-02).

That last point matters here. The FAIR Plan's dwelling policy is a named-peril policy covering fire, lightning, internal explosion and smoke (California FAIR Plan). It doesn't cover water damage, theft or liability. Those come from a separate Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy, which the FAIR Plan doesn't sell (California FAIR Plan, DIC). The Department of Insurance says only about half of FAIR Plan policyholders carry one (CDI fact sheet). So when your FAIR Plan premium rises, remember your true insurance cost is both bills together.

How can I lower my FAIR Plan or homeowners insurance premium?

Wildfire-mitigation discounts are required. Under the state's "Safer from Wildfires" framework, insurers must offer discounts for specific home-hardening steps, including a Class A fire-rated roof, clearing combustibles within 5 feet of the house, ember- and fire-resistant vents, double-pane windows or shutters, enclosed eaves, and moving sheds at least 30 feet away. Neighborhoods that become Firewise USA communities can earn community-level discounts. The FAIR Plan offers these discounts too, through a broker registered with the plan (CDI, Insurers Currently Offering Discounts). Insurers set their own discount amounts, so it pays to compare.

The private market is slowly reopening. On August 3, 2026, the Department reported that FAIR Plan growth slowed to 1.9% in the second quarter, the third straight quarter of slowing growth, and that about 24,000 policies left the plan in April and May (CDI, August 3, 2026). That doesn't mean a private carrier will write your home, but it's worth asking every year.

What if I'm buying a home in a wildfire area right now?

Get insurance quotes in the first few days of escrow, not the last week. In fire-prone parts of LA County, the Inland Empire foothills and the Sierra, insurance can change what a home really costs to own as much as the interest rate does. For context, Freddie Mac's national average 30-year fixed rate was 7.40% for the week of October 15, 2026, unchanged from the week before and up from 6.27% a year earlier (Freddie Mac PMMS). That's a national survey average, not a quote. With financing costs already elevated, a 29% swing in insurance can be the difference between a payment that fits and one that doesn't, so build it into your budget before you remove contingencies.

If you're weighing whether to harden and stay, remodel or move, my guide to buying, remodeling or rebuilding in California walks through the trade-offs.

Reading from outside California? The FAIR Plan is California-specific, but most states have a similar insurer of last resort, and the escrow rules above are federal, so they apply wherever your mortgage is.

Frequently asked questions

When does the FAIR Plan rate increase take effect?

For new and renewing FAIR Plan dwelling policies starting October 15, 2026. If your policy renews later, the new rate applies at that renewal.

Will my mortgage payment go up right away?

Usually not the same month. Your servicer pays the renewal bill from escrow, then adjusts your monthly escrow amount at the next escrow analysis. Any shortage of one month's payment or more can be spread over at least 12 months.

Will every FAIR Plan policyholder pay 29% more?

No. 29.1% is the average. Higher-risk wildfire areas will see larger increases, and some lower-risk policyholders will see decreases.

Does the FAIR Plan offer discounts for wildfire mitigation?

Yes. Under California's Safer from Wildfires framework, the FAIR Plan and private insurers offer discounts for steps like a Class A roof, ember-resistant vents and a cleared 5-foot zone around the home.

Can I refinance with FAIR Plan insurance?

Generally yes, if your total coverage meets the loan's requirements. That often means pairing the FAIR Plan with a Difference in Conditions policy so all required perils are covered.
