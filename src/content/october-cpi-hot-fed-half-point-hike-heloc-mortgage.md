**The short answer:** October's consumer prices rose 0.4% and core prices rose 0.3%, both a tenth above what economists expected, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The same day, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said that with core inflation at that pace, "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting." Fixed mortgage rates moved first: Mortgage News Daily's index jumped to 7.30%. If you carry a prime-based HELOC, a December hike would likely raise your rate soon after; if you're in escrow, this is a good week to talk about your lock.

*As of November 11, 2026. Rates cited are published national indexes and averages, not rates offered by Emory Financial. Markets can move during the day.*

## What did the October inflation report actually say?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on November 10 that the Consumer Price Index "increased 0.4 percent on a seasonally adjusted basis in October," after 0.6% in September. Core prices (everything except food and energy) "rose 0.3 percent in October, as it did in September and August." Shelter rose 0.3%, energy 0.7% and food 0.5% ([BLS, CPI October 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)).

Economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected 0.3% overall and 0.2% for core, so both came in a tenth hotter ([StockTitan, November 10, 2026](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/october-2026-cpi-inflation-report)). A tenth of a percent sounds small. For the Fed, three straight months of 0.3% core is the point, because that pace works out to well above its 2% goal over a year.

One quirk: you won't see an official "inflation is X% over the past year" number for October. BLS says it "cannot publish 12-month percent changes for October 2026" for most indexes because October 2025 prices weren't collected during the federal funding lapse last fall.

## Why are people suddenly talking about a half-point hike?

Because a Fed governor said so, out loud, hours before the report. Speaking in London on November 10, Governor Waller said he "would still support a 25 basis point increase" in December. But if core inflation came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher," he said, "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting," adding that "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk" ([Federal Reserve, Waller speech, November 10, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20261110a.htm)). Core came in at exactly 0.3%.

He wasn't alone. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the same day that "the FOMC needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases" and that "it may be possible to move more rapidly this winter" ([Kitco, November 10, 2026](http://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-11-10/feds-waller-and-logan-signal-readiness-hike-higher-and-faster-50-bps-table)). After the report, futures markets priced about an 85% chance of a hike at the December 8–9 meeting, according to CME FedWatch data cited by Mortgage Professional ([MPA, November 10, 2026](https://www.mpamag.com/us/mortgage-industry/industry-trends/mortgage-rates-set-to-rise-as-october-cpi-tops-forecasts/596855)). That's a market probability, not a Fed decision and not my forecast. The Fed's target is 3.75%–4.00% today.

## What happened to mortgage rates?

They rose right away. Mortgage News Daily's index of 30-year fixed rates for top-tier borrowers jumped 0.12 percentage point on November 10 to 7.30%, after weeks of easing. The 10-year Treasury yield, which fixed mortgage rates tend to follow, rose about 0.09 point to roughly 4.98% ([Mortgage News Daily, November 10, 2026](https://www.mortgagenewsdaily.com/markets/mortgage-rates-11102026)). For context, Freddie Mac's weekly national average was 7.18% on November 5 ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)); its next survey comes out Thursday, November 12, and will be the first to reflect this report.

Last week I wrote that [a hot jobs report could stall the rate dip](/insights/mortgage-rates-fall-jobs-report-lock-or-float-november-2026). The inflation report did more than stall it. Mortgage News Daily noted the Fed has been more focused on inflation than on jobs, which is why this report hit harder.

## I have a HELOC. What does a December hike mean for me?

Most HELOCs are variable, typically the prime rate plus a margin. Prime was 7.00% as of November 9 ([Federal Reserve H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/)), and banks generally move prime when the Fed changes its target. So a quarter-point hike would likely raise a prime-based HELOC by about a quarter point soon after; a half-point hike, about half a point. During the draw period, many HELOCs are interest-only, so a rate change shows up in your payment right away.

Three things to do now, before the December 8–9 meeting:

1. **Find your index and margin** on your HELOC agreement or statement, so you know exactly how a change flows through.
2. **Check whether you can fix part of the balance.** The CFPB notes some HELOCs let you convert part of the balance to a fixed rate ([CFPB HELOC booklet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_heloc-brochure.pdf)). It usually starts higher than the variable rate, but it makes the payment predictable.
3. **Decide what to pay down first.** If you have spare cash and a variable balance, that balance is the one exposed to further hikes.

I walked through the decision in more detail after the October meeting in [The Fed held rates today](/insights/fed-holds-rates-october-2026-heloc-mortgage).

## I'm in escrow. Lock or float?

I don't predict rates. But with fixed rates up sharply in a day and two Fed officials talking about moving faster, the risk of floating went up this week. A lock protects you from market increases, though the CFPB notes your rate can still change if your loan type, down payment, appraisal, credit or documented income changes ([CFPB, What is a rate lock?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-lock-in-rate-en-143/)). Match the lock to a realistic closing date, with the Thanksgiving and December holidays in mind, and ask what an extension costs.

## FAQ

**Did inflation go up or down in October?** Prices rose 0.4% for the month, a smaller jump than September's 0.6% but more than the 0.3% economists expected. Core prices rose 0.3% for the third month in a row, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

**Why isn't there a year-over-year inflation number for October?** BLS didn't collect October 2025 prices during the federal funding lapse, so it says it can't publish 12-month changes for October 2026 for most indexes.

**Will the Fed raise rates in December?** No one knows until December 9. Governor Waller supports at least a quarter-point hike and said a half-point should be considered; markets put the odds of a hike near 85% after the report, according to CME FedWatch data cited by Mortgage Professional.

**Will my HELOC rate go up?** If it's tied to prime and the Fed raises its target, very likely, by about the same amount, soon after prime changes. Check your agreement for your index, margin and any rate caps.

**Does a Fed hike raise my existing fixed mortgage rate?** No. A fixed rate doesn't change. New fixed mortgage rates follow the bond market, which often moves before the Fed does, as it did on November 10.
