**The short answer:** Early returns from California's November 3, 2026 election show voters approving both housing measures. The Associated Press called Proposition 37, the $25 billion down payment loan program for newly built homes, on election night, and projected that Proposition 1, the $11.25 billion housing bond, will pass. Results stay unofficial until the Secretary of State certifies them on December 11, 2026. Even after that, neither measure puts money in buyers' hands right away: the state still has to issue bonds and write the program rules, and no start date has been announced.

*As of the morning of November 4, 2026. Results are unofficial and ballots are still being counted. This article reports the outcome; it does not take a side on either measure.*

## What did voters decide on Prop 1 and Prop 37?

Here's where things stood in the early count, from the outlets that reported it:

- **Prop 37 (down payment loans for new homes): passed.** The AP called it at 9:06 p.m. Pacific on election night, according to KQED. KQED showed 61% yes and 38.9% no, with an estimated 58.41% of votes counted.
- **Prop 1 (housing bond): projected to pass.** CalMatters, via LAist, reported 58% yes with about 42% of ballots counted on election night. 10News San Diego later reported that the AP projects it will pass.

Two other statewide measures that touch housing were trailing in early returns, and neither had been called:

- **Prop 45** (deadlines for environmental review, CEQA, on many housing and infrastructure projects): KQED reported 54.3% no as of about 10 p.m. election night and called it "still too close to call."
- **Prop 43** (a two-thirds vote for local special taxes that citizens put on the ballot): KQED showed 54.9% no, with an estimated 57.73% counted, as of 2:55 a.m. Pacific on November 4.

Under the California Constitution, an initiative statute approved by voters, like Prop 37, takes effect on the fifth day after the Secretary of State files the statement of the vote. Taking effect means the law exists. It doesn't mean a loan program is open.

## When could Prop 37 down payment loans actually be available?

No date has been set. Here's what the Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) says the program involves:

- **CalHFA runs it** and decides how much of the up-to-$25 billion in revenue bonds to sell, and over what period.
- **The loan covers up to 17% of the home price**, and the buyer puts down at least 3%.
- **Household income can be up to twice the area's typical (median) income.**
- **Only newly built homes qualify**, the buyer has to be the first purchaser, and price limits apply.
- **Borrowers repay the loan monthly**, and those payments repay the bonds. The LAO estimates no direct state or local cost.

The LAO also lists real unknowns: how much investor demand there will be for the bonds, how the loan's cost will compare with other down payment options, and whether the program leads to more homes being built and bought. CalMatters notes that experts have questioned how this first-of-its-kind program would work, and that critics question whether privately financed loans will be affordable for the buyers who need help most. The interest rate and term will be set by CalHFA.

For the full breakdown, including who qualifies and how the monthly payment on the second loan affects your debt-to-income ratio, see [Prop 37 on California's November Ballot](/insights/california-prop-37-new-home-down-payment-loan).

## What does Prop 1 mean for buyers?

If Prop 1's approval holds, the state can borrow $11.25 billion through bonds: $10 billion repaid from the state budget, and $1.25 billion for veterans repaid by veterans' loan payments. According to the LAO's breakdown, most of it goes to affordable rental housing, about $1.1 billion to homeownership help for low- and moderate-income first-time buyers, and $1.25 billion to the veterans home loan program. The official voter guide didn't spell out income limits or the amount of help per buyer, so watch for those as the state programs set their rules. I covered the full breakdown in [Prop 1 on Your November 3 Ballot](/insights/california-prop-1-housing-bond-buyers-renters).

## Should I wait for these programs before I buy?

For most people, no. I'd keep preparing while the details come together:

- **If you're shopping for a resale home,** Prop 37 doesn't apply to you. It's limited to new construction.
- **If a new-build community appeals to you,** go ahead and tour. Just don't count on Prop 37 money in a contract until CalHFA has opened the program and published its terms.
- **Ask about what's available today.** CalHFA and many cities and counties already offer down payment assistance, and family gifts are another route (see [Helping Your Adult Child Buy a Home](/insights/help-adult-child-buy-home-gift-cosign)).
- **Get your file ready.** Your credit, income documents and savings history are the same whichever program you end up using.

**Outside California?** Both measures apply only to homes in California. If you're relocating or helping family buy here, watch for how the new programs will define residency.

## FAQ

**Did Prop 37 pass?** Yes, according to the Associated Press, which called the race on election night. KQED showed about 61% yes with an estimated 58% of votes counted. The result becomes official when the Secretary of State certifies it on December 11, 2026.

**Did Prop 1 pass?** The AP projects that it will pass, based on early results. CalMatters reported 58% yes with about 42% of ballots counted on election night. Counting continues.

**Can I apply for a Prop 37 loan now?** No. CalHFA still has to issue bonds and set the program's rates, terms and price limits, and no launch date has been announced.

**Does Prop 37 work for a resale home?** No. Only newly built homes (and certain newly converted units) where you are the first buyer qualify, according to the LAO and CalMatters.

**When will California's election results be final?** Counties have until December 3, 2026 to certify, and the Secretary of State certifies the Statement of Vote on December 11, 2026.
