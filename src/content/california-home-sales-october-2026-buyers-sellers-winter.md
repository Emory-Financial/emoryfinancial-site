**The short answer:** California sold existing single-family homes at a 246,830 annual pace in October 2026, down 12.7% from a year earlier and the lowest level in nearly three years, according to the California Association of Realtors. But the statewide median price, $886,760, was essentially flat from last year, and the typical home still sold in 29 days. Fewer buyers are shopping, not fewer sellers holding firm, so this winter your leverage is more about terms and timing than a big price cut. With Fed officials signaling more rate hikes, plan your financing before you plan your offer.

*As of November 19, 2026. Sales and price figures are C.A.R.'s October 2026 report. Mortgage rates cited are Freddie Mac national averages, not rates offered by Emory Financial.*

## What happened to California home sales in October?

They fell hard. C.A.R. reported on November 17 that statewide sales dropped 5.2% from September and 12.7% from October 2025, to a 246,830 seasonally adjusted annual pace ([C.A.R., November 17, 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/california-home-sales-fall-to-nearly-three-year-low-car-reports-302942636.html)). A month ago, when [I wrote about September's slowdown](/insights/california-buyers-leverage-seller-credits-fall-2026), sales were down 6.5% from a year earlier. The decline nearly doubled in a month.

C.A.R. President Mark Peterson tied it to financing costs: "When mortgage rates jumped to a nearly three-year high in late September, we saw many buyers step back." C.A.R. puts October's average 30-year fixed rate at 7.33%, based on Freddie Mac data, up from 6.86% in September and 6.25% a year earlier.

The pain was in the middle of the market. Six of seven price segments fell by double digits from a year ago. Homes between $750,000 and $999,999 dropped 20.7%, and the $500,000 to $749,999 range, about a quarter of all sales, fell 16.2%. Only homes priced at $2 million and above grew, up 0.7%. That's the segment where buyers are least dependent on a mortgage rate.

## If sales fell this much, why didn't prices drop?

Because sellers aren't rushing. The statewide median was $886,760, down 1.4% from September and just 0.1% below October 2025. Sellers got 98.5% of their list price on average, slightly more than a year ago (98.3%), and median days on market was 29, faster than last October's 32.

Supply did loosen a bit. Active listings were up 6.6% from a year ago, and C.A.R.'s Unsold Inventory Index, the months it would take to sell every listing at the current pace, rose to 3.8 from 3.2 a year earlier. So there are more homes to choose from and less competition for each one, but sellers who are priced right are still selling.

## How did Los Angeles and the rest of Southern California do?

Southern California fell harder than the state. Regional sales were down 18.9% from a year earlier, and the Los Angeles metro median slipped 2.5% to $827,750. In Los Angeles County itself, sales fell 17.5% from a year earlier, and the median dropped 4.0% from September to $955,090, though that's only 0.6% below last October.

The Bay Area is a different market. Sales there fell 12.1% from a year earlier, but the median rose 5.8% to $1,375,000, and homes in Santa Clara and San Mateo counties sold in a median of 11 days. Inventory is under 2.5 months in most Bay Area counties, compared with 4 to 5 months in much of Southern California.

## Are mortgage rates going to make this worse?

That depends on inflation, and the Fed has been clear about which way it's leaning. Minutes from the Fed's October 27–28 meeting, released yesterday, say "almost all participants" judged that further action to raise the federal funds rate "would likely be appropriate" to return inflation to 2%. "A couple of participants" said an increase of more than a quarter point "could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavorable." Fed staff also noted that "residential mortgage activity declined further amid the sharp rise in mortgage rates" ([Federal Reserve, FOMC minutes, October 27–28, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm)). The next meeting is December 8–9.

Fixed mortgage rates don't follow the Fed's rate directly, but they do react to inflation and Fed expectations. Freddie Mac's national 30-year average was 7.11% for the week of November 12, down from 7.18% the week before but up from 6.24% a year earlier ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). Freddie Mac's next weekly survey comes out today at noon Eastern. I covered what a December hike would mean for home equity lines in [my article on October's inflation report](/insights/october-cpi-hot-fed-half-point-hike-heloc-mortgage).

## I'm buying. Should I shop this winter or wait?

I can't tell you where prices or rates go next, and C.A.R.'s chief economist Jordan Levine expects sales to stay soft in the near term while "pent-up demand continues to build." What I can tell you is what a slower winter tends to offer a ready buyer:

- **Less competition.** More inventory and fewer buyers mean fewer multiple-offer situations, especially in Southern California and the Inland Empire.
- **Room on terms.** Sellers holding firm on price may still pay toward your closing costs or a rate buydown. Loan rules cap how much; my [seller credits guide](/insights/california-buyers-leverage-seller-credits-fall-2026) has the limits.
- **Time to inspect and negotiate.** Longer days on market give you room for inspection and repair requests that a hot market doesn't.

The trade-off is rate risk. If you wait for a lower price and rates rise in the meantime, the payment can end up higher anyway. That's why I'd get fully pre-approved now, know your top payment, and be ready to lock once you're in contract.

## I'm selling. What should I take from this?

Price for the market you're in, not the one from 2024. Homes are still selling in about a month statewide when they're priced right, but with more listings to compare against, buyers will skip a home that looks overpriced. In Southern California, where inventory runs 4 to 5 months, expect buyers to ask for credits. Offering a credit toward their closing costs or rate can sometimes net you more than an equivalent price cut. If you're selling to buy your next home, talk to your lender about both sides at once so your timing and financing line up.

## FAQ

**How much did California home sales drop in October 2026?** Existing single-family home sales fell 12.7% from October 2025 and 5.2% from September, to a 246,830 seasonally adjusted annual pace, according to C.A.R. It was the lowest sales pace in nearly three years.

**What is the median home price in California right now?** C.A.R. reported a statewide median of $886,760 for October 2026, down 1.4% from September and 0.1% from a year earlier. In Los Angeles County the median was $955,090.

**Is it a buyer's market in California?** It depends on where you're shopping. Southern California and the Inland Empire have 4 to 5 months of inventory and longer days on market, which gives buyers more room. Most Bay Area counties still have under 2.5 months of inventory and homes selling in a few weeks or less.

**Will mortgage rates go up in December?** No one knows yet. Minutes from the Fed's October meeting show almost all officials expected further rate increases would likely be appropriate, and the next decision is December 9. Fixed mortgage rates follow the bond market and can move before or after the Fed acts.

**Can a seller help pay my closing costs?** Usually, yes, within limits set by your loan type and down payment. A seller credit can cover closing costs, prepaid items and rate buydowns, but not your down payment.
