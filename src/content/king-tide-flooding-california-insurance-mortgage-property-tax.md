**The short answer:** Record king tides, amplified by this winter's super El Niño, flooded homes and streets along the California coast this week, from Pacifica and Marin to Long Beach and La Jolla. If water got into your home, document everything before you clean up and call your insurer right away, because most homeowners policies exclude flooding and an NFIP flood claim has a 60-day documentation window. Tell your mortgage servicer too: insurance checks usually name your lender. And if the damage cut your home's value by $10,000 or more, you can ask your county assessor for a temporary property tax reduction within 12 months.

*As of November 27, 2026. Tide figures are as reported by ABC News on November 26. More king tides are forecast for December 23–25 and January 21–22.*

## What happened this week?

The highest tides of the year arrived on top of an unusually warm ocean. In San Francisco Bay, water levels hit a record 8.23 feet on Tuesday and 8.21 feet on Wednesday, beating the old record of 7.97 feet set in 1982. Santa Barbara set an all-time record of 8.21 feet on Wednesday, and Los Angeles Harbor reached 8.19 feet, tying the record set just last month ([ABC News, November 26, 2026](https://abcnews.com/US/king-tides-pummel-california-coast-leaders-fear/story?id=138466654)).

## Will my homeowners insurance pay for this?

Probably not for the flooding itself. The California Department of Insurance says homeowners, renters and commercial policies "typically exclude damage caused by flooding, mudslides, debris flows, and other similar disasters" unless a recent wildfire or another covered peril caused them. Policies "may provide coverage for certain water damage caused by wind-driven rain," and damage from fallen trees "is generally covered under all standard homeowner policies" ([CDI, September 22, 2026](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0100-press-releases/release033-2026.cfm)).

Rising seawater coming through the garage is flood. Rain blown through a broken window may not be. If you aren't sure which you have, file the claim anyway and let the insurer decide. A denial in writing is better than a guess. CDI's consumer hotline is 800-927-4357.

A few details worth knowing. Your insurer "may offer up to $5,000 without an adjuster's visit," and up to $20,000 with authorization and documentation; that advance comes out of your final payment. FloodSmart says claims usually take "4-8 weeks" to finalize, standard NFIP policies don't cover mold, and basement coverage is limited ([FloodSmart, Start a Claim](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how-do-i-start-my-flood-claim)). The adjuster "should never ask you for money or collect your deductible amount" ([FEMA, File Your Claim](https://www.fema.gov/nfip-file-your-claim)). If your claim is denied, you can appeal to FEMA within 60 days of the insurer's letter ([NFIP Claims Checklist, PDF](https://agents.floodsmart.gov/sites/default/files/media/document/2026-05/NFIP%20Claims%20Checklist%20for%20Policyholders.pdf)).

## Why does my mortgage company need to know?

Because it's probably going to be on the check. FloodSmart says that if you have a mortgage, the claim check is made out to you and your mortgage company ([FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/how-do-i-start-my-flood-claim)). The NFIP checklist also tells policyholders to confirm the right mortgage company is listed on their declarations page "to avoid payment delays." If your loan was transferred to a new servicer recently, check that now.

For larger claims, your servicer may hold the money and release it in stages as repairs are done. California law requires lenders to pay interest on insurance money they hold while you repair, and I walked through how that works in [what your mortgage company owes you on insurance payouts](/insights/la-fire-insurance-proceeds-mortgage-lender-report).

If the damage or the repair bills put your next payment at risk, call your servicer before you miss one and ask what options it has for homeowners affected by a disaster. It's a much easier conversation before the payment is late. My guide to [what to do if you fall behind on your mortgage](/insights/behind-on-mortgage-california-options-foreclosure-rights) covers your options and rights in California.

## Can I get a break on my property taxes?

Possibly, and many people miss it. Under California Revenue and Taxation Code section 170, if a "misfortune or calamity" damages your property through no fault of your own, you can ask the county assessor to reassess it. The State Board of Equalization says flooding counts, every county has adopted the required ordinance, and "the loss estimate must be at least $10,000 of current market value" ([BOE, Disaster Relief](https://www.boe.ca.gov/proptaxes/disaster-relief.htm); [R&T Code § 170](https://california.public.law/codes/revenue_and_taxation_code_section_170)).

What you need to know:

- **Deadline:** file within the time your county ordinance allows or 12 months from the damage, whichever is later.
- **No governor's declaration needed** for this basic relief. That's a separate category in the law.
- **How the break works:** the tax is prorated from the month of the damage until the end of the fiscal year or until repairs are finished, whichever comes first. Any overpayment is refunded without a separate refund claim, but the BOE says "you must still pay your regular tax bill."
- **When you rebuild:** if you repair or rebuild "in a like or similar manner, the property will retain its prior value (Proposition 13)." New square footage or extras are added at market value.

One thing the basic claim doesn't do is delay the December 10 installment. The BOE says a deferral without penalty is available only for property "substantially damaged or destroyed in a Governor-proclaimed disaster," and not when taxes are paid through an impound account. None of the coverage I read this week reported a new proclamation for the king tide flooding, so plan to pay on time unless your county tells you otherwise. My earlier piece on [the 2026–27 property tax bill and its deadlines](/insights/la-county-property-tax-bill-2026-27-deadlines) has the installment dates.

## More king tides are coming. Is it too late to buy flood insurance?

For the next round, probably. CDI says flood insurance "generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, except in the case of a home purchase where flood insurance is required by the lender" ([CDI](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0100-press-releases/release033-2026.cfm)). Forecasters expect the next king tides on December 23–25 and January 21–22, and say they could rival this week's records ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/king-tides-pummel-california-coast-leaders-fear/story?id=138466654)).

## I'm buying near the coast. What should I ask?

Ask the seller's agent whether the home took water this week, and read the seller's disclosures carefully for any flooding history. Look up the flood zone on FEMA's [Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/howto) and get a flood quote during your contingency period. A policy bought in connection with your loan can start at closing, which is one of the few ways around the 30-day wait. The premium becomes part of your monthly housing cost, so it affects what you qualify for.

## FAQ

**Does homeowners insurance cover king tide flooding in California?** Generally no. The California Department of Insurance says homeowners and renters policies typically exclude flooding unless it was caused by a recent wildfire or another covered peril. Some water damage from wind-driven rain may be covered, so file a claim if you're unsure.

**How long do I have to file an NFIP flood claim?** Report the loss as soon as possible. FEMA's claims checklist says to submit your supporting documentation within 60 days of the flood, or within the time your adjuster or insurer gives you.

**Will the flood insurance check be made out to my mortgage company?** Usually, yes. FloodSmart says that if you have a mortgage, the check is written to you and your mortgage company, so confirm your current servicer is on your policy.

**Can I lower my property taxes after flood damage in California?** If the damage reduced your property's value by at least $10,000, you can file a calamity reassessment claim with your county assessor within 12 months (or longer if your county allows). The tax is prorated, and your prior Prop 13 value returns once you rebuild in a like or similar manner.

**Can I buy flood insurance before the December king tides?** An NFIP policy bought on its own generally takes 30 days to start, so a policy bought now wouldn't be in force for December 23–25 but could be by the January tides. Private flood policies set their own waiting periods.
