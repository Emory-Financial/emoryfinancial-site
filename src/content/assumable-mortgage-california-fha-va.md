**The short answer:** Every FHA-insured loan, and VA-guaranteed loans, can be taken over by a qualified buyer, along with the seller's original rate and remaining term. That can be valuable when about three in four California owners with a mortgage have a rate under 5% and new loans average above 7% nationally. The catches: you must qualify with the loan servicer, you usually have to cover the gap between the price and the loan balance in cash or with a second loan, and it can take weeks longer than a normal purchase. Most conventional fixed-rate loans can't be assumed by a new buyer at all.

*As of November 16, 2026. Rate figures are national averages from Freddie Mac. Program rules come from HUD, VA and Fannie Mae pages cited below.*

## Why is this worth a look right now?

Rates turned back up last week. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 5.15%, its biggest weekly gain since April 2025, after producer prices came in 6.5% higher than a year earlier ([The Truth About Mortgage, November 13, 2026](https://www.thetruthaboutmortgage.com/mortgage-rates-climb-back-toward-7-50-as-10-year-yield-sees-biggest-weekly-jump-in-over-a-year/)). Mortgage rates tend to follow that yield. Freddie Mac's national average 30-year fixed rate was 7.11% for the week of November 12, down for the fourth week in a row, but well above 6.24% a year earlier ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). Thursday's survey will show whether last week's bond selloff reached mortgages.

Meanwhile, California's Legislative Analyst's Office says about 75% of California homeowners had a mortgage rate below 5% as of September 2026, and that an owner with a 5% loan who bought a similarly priced home at current rates would face a monthly payment about 17% higher ([LAO](https://lao.ca.gov/LAOEconTax/Article/Detail/793)). That gap is the whole appeal of an assumption: the buyer steps into the seller's rate instead of the market's.

The government expects more of these. In an October 20, 2026 Federal Register notice, VA raised its estimate of veterans filing the form to sell a home by assumption from 1,000 to 5,000 a year. It attributed the increase to "assumable low-interest-rate loans in a high-interest-rate environment" ([Federal Register, FR Doc. 2026-21454](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-21454.pdf)).

**FHA.** HUD's handbook says plainly that "all FHA-insured Mortgages are assumable" ([HUD Handbook 4000.1, III.A.3.b, as reproduced by ACES](https://acesiq.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/AI/pages/609517973/III.A.3.b.+Assumptions+03+14+16)). For loans made since December 15, 1989, the buyer must be found creditworthy, and transfers to buyers who won't live in the home are generally barred, with narrow exceptions ([HUD Handbook 4330.1, Chapter 6](https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/43301c6hsgh.pdf)). In other words, this is a path for people buying a home to live in, not for investors.

**VA.** You don't have to be a veteran to assume a VA loan. But the seller should understand what happens to their VA entitlement. VA's own acknowledgment form tells sellers their entitlement "will still be tied to the loan unless the assumption includes a SOE," a substitution of entitlement, and that this is only possible when the buyer is an eligible veteran with enough entitlement ([VA Form 26-10291](https://tenaco.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/VA-Form-26-10291-Assumption-Entitlement-Acknowledgment.pdf)). Otherwise, it isn't restored until the loan is paid in full. For a veteran who wants to use a VA loan again soon, that matters. I covered how VA loans work here in [this Veterans Day piece](/insights/va-home-loan-california-veterans-2026).

**Conventional.** Most Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans carry a due-on-sale clause. Fannie Mae's servicing guide authorizes transfers to a new buyer mainly for adjustable-rate loans, and not during an extended fixed-rate period ([Fannie Mae Servicing Guide D1-4.2-02](https://servicing-guide.fanniemae.com/svc/d1-4.2-02/conventional-mortgage-loans-include-due-sale-or-due-transfer-provision)). Separately, some transfers don't trigger the clause at all, such as to a spouse in a divorce, to a child or other relative who will live there, or to the survivor when a joint owner dies ([Fannie Mae Servicing Guide D1-4.1-02](https://guide-servicing.fanniemae.com/svc/d1-4.1-02/allowable-exemptions-due-type-transfer)). That's often how a family home stays in the family, which I wrote about in [divorce and the family home](/insights/divorce-family-home-california).

## What's the catch? The equity gap

In California, where many sellers have built up years of equity, that gap is often the deal breaker. Some buyers cover part of it with a second mortgage, which needs its own approval and has its own (usually higher) rate. Whether the blended result still beats a new first mortgage depends on the size of the gap, so run both versions before you fall in love with the low rate.

## What does it cost, and how long does it take?

- **FHA:** HUD doubled the maximum fee a servicer may charge to process an assumption from $900 to $1,800 in 2024 ([National Association of Realtors](https://www.nar.realtor/news/washington-report/fha-increases-allowable-fees-for-assumable-loans)). Credit reports and employment verifications can be charged separately.
- **VA:** The servicer's processing fee is capped at $300, plus certain other permitted charges ([VA Circular 26-23-10, Change 1](https://www.tenaco.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/VA-Circular-26-23-10-Change-1-02-27-24.pdf); [VA bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USVAVBA/bulletins/38d7eba)). The buyer also pays a VA funding fee of 0.5% of the loan balance unless exempt ([VA.gov](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/funding-fee-and-closing-costs/)).
- **Timing:** HUD's servicing handbook gives the servicer 45 days to finish the creditworthiness review once it has every document. Realtors report assumptions often take 30 to 60 days or more, so build that into your contract dates.

An assumable loan is one of the few ways to buy below today's market rate without a seller credit or buydown, and right now it can be worth asking about on every FHA or VA listing. ## FAQ

**Can anyone assume an FHA loan?** No. The buyer has to be approved by the servicer as creditworthy and, with narrow exceptions, has to live in the home. Investors generally can't assume FHA loans.

**Do I have to be a veteran to assume a VA loan?** No. But if you aren't an eligible veteran substituting your own entitlement, the seller's VA entitlement stays tied to the loan until it's paid off.

**Can I assume a conventional loan?** Usually not if it's a fixed-rate loan with a due-on-sale clause. Fannie Mae allows servicers to approve transfers mainly on adjustable-rate loans. Transfers to a spouse, child or other relative who will live there are treated differently.

**Do I need cash for the difference between the price and the loan balance?** Yes, or a second loan. You only take over the remaining balance, so the rest of the price, plus closing costs, has to come from somewhere.

**How long does a mortgage assumption take?** Plan on longer than a normal purchase. HUD gives FHA servicers 45 days after receiving every document, and Realtors report 30 to 60 days or more is common.
