**The short answer:** The Federal Housing Finance Agency's latest report, released November 5, shows early-stage late payments on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans rose to 1.03% of loans in August, and foreclosure starts jumped 18% from July. If you're one of the homeowners falling behind, the most important thing to know is that you have more time and more options than it feels like: federal rules generally bar a foreclosure filing until you're at least 120 days behind, and California law adds its own contact requirements and waiting periods. The homeowners who keep their homes are almost always the ones who call their servicer early.

*As of November 8, 2026. National figures cover Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans only and come from FHFA's August 2026 Foreclosure Prevention report. This is general information, not legal advice.*

## What did the new report show?

FHFA publishes a monthly report on the loans Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac back, which is a large share of all U.S. mortgages. The August numbers, published November 5, show more borrowers slipping ([FHFA, August 2026 report](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/d/fprp/foreclosure-prevention-refinance-and-fpm-report-august-2026)):

- **30 to 59 days late:** 313,254 loans, or 1.03% of loans, up from 292,965 (0.96%) in July.
- **60 or more days late:** 256,999 loans (0.84%), up from 249,816 (0.82%).
- **Serious delinquency:** 0.60%, up from 0.59%.
- **Foreclosure starts:** 8,930, up 18.0% from 7,569.

## What options can my servicer offer?

Your servicer is the company you send your payment to. For loans owned by Fannie Mae, these are the main ways to stay in the home ([Fannie Mae, "We're here to help"](https://fanniemae.com/here-help-single-family)):

- **Forbearance:** an agreement "to pause or reduce monthly mortgage payments for a certain period" while you resolve a short-term hardship. After a disaster, Fannie Mae says forbearance can last up to 12 months.
- **Repayment plan:** for a temporary hardship that's now resolved. You repay the missed amounts over up to twelve months, on top of your regular payment.
- **Payment deferral:** missed principal and interest are moved to the end of the loan as a non-interest-bearing balance, due when you sell, refinance or pay off the loan. Your regular payment then continues as before.
- **Loan modification:** a permanent change to the original terms, "such as monthly payment amount, loan term, or interest rate."
- **Reinstatement:** if you can catch up all at once, you pay the missed amounts and the loan is current again.

If keeping the home isn't realistic, a **short sale** or a **mortgage release** (voluntarily transferring the home to the loan's owner) can avoid a foreclosure. FHA, VA, jumbo and private loans have their own menus, so ask your servicer exactly which options apply to your loan.

## What does California law require before a foreclosure?

California has some of the strongest borrower protections in the country, much of it from the Homeowner Bill of Rights that took effect in 2013. According to the Attorney General's office, it restricts "dual tracking" (foreclosing while a loan modification is under discussion) and guarantees "a single point of contact with knowledge of their loan" ([California Attorney General](https://oag.ca.gov/node/37588)).

A few specifics from the Civil Code:

- **Contact first.** Before recording a notice of default, the servicer must contact you by phone or in person to assess your finances and explore ways to avoid foreclosure, tell you that you can ask for a follow-up meeting within 14 days, and give you the HUD number for finding a housing counselor. It then generally has to wait 30 days ([Civil Code § 2923.5](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_2923.5)).
- **No foreclosure steps while a complete application is pending.** If you submit a complete first-lien loan modification application at least five business days before a scheduled sale, the servicer can't record a notice of default or notice of sale, or hold the sale, while it's under review. If you're denied, you get at least 30 days to appeal ([Civil Code § 2923.6](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_2923.6)).
- **Built-in waiting periods.** At least three months must pass after a notice of default is recorded, and the sale date can be no earlier than three months and 20 days after it ([Civil Code § 2924](https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_2924)).
- **A right to catch up.** You can generally reinstate the loan until five business days before the sale date in the first recorded notice of sale ([Civil Code § 2924c](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_2924c)).

Not every protection applies to every loan or servicer; the statutes have their own limits. A HUD-approved housing counselor or a real estate attorney can tell you which apply to yours.

## How do I avoid foreclosure scams?

Fear is what scammers sell. California's Department of Financial Protection and Innovation is blunt: "Do NOT pay anyone asking for upfront/advance fees for loan modification services or mortgage forbearance services." It says advance fees for loan modifications are illegal in California, warns against anyone who tells you to sign your home's title over to them, and warns against advice to stop paying on purpose to qualify for a modification ([DFPI alert](https://dfpi.ca.gov/alert/mortgage-modification-and-foreclosure-scams-alert/)). Report suspected scams to the California Department of Real Estate at 877-373-4542.

Free help is real. The CFPB can connect you with a HUD-approved housing counselor at (855) 411-CFPB (2372) ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/i-cant-make-my-mortgage-payments-how-long-will-it-take-before-ill-face-foreclosure-en-1849/)).

## FAQ

**How late can I be before foreclosure starts?** The CFPB says that generally, the legal foreclosure process can't start until you're at least 120 days behind. In California, the servicer must also contact you, or make diligent attempts to, and then wait 30 days before recording a notice of default.

**Can my lender foreclose while I'm applying for a loan modification?** Under California Civil Code § 2923.6, if you submit a complete first-lien modification application at least five business days before a scheduled sale, the servicer can't record a notice of default or notice of sale, or hold the sale, while it's pending. If you're denied, you have at least 30 days to appeal. The statute has limits, so confirm that it covers your loan.

**What's the difference between forbearance and a payment deferral?** Forbearance pauses or reduces payments for a set period during a hardship. A payment deferral, as Fannie Mae describes it, moves missed principal and interest to the end of the loan as a non-interest-bearing balance due when you sell, refinance or pay off the loan.

**Can I still catch up after a notice of default?** Generally yes. California Civil Code § 2924c lets you reinstate until five business days before the sale date in the first recorded notice of sale.

**Is it legal to charge me upfront for loan modification help?** No. California's DFPI says advance fees for loan modification services are illegal in California. Free help is available from HUD-approved housing counselors.
