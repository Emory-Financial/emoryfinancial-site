**The short answer:** On November 24, FHFA set the 2027 conforming loan limit for a one-unit home at $855,400 in most of the country, up $22,650 from 2026, and at $1,283,100 in the highest-cost areas, including Los Angeles, Orange, San Francisco, San Mateo, Alameda and Santa Clara counties. The new limits take effect January 1, 2027. Both numbers came in above the early estimates several big lenders had been using since September. If your loan amount sits just above your county's 2026 limit, you may now fit a conforming loan instead of a jumbo, depending on timing and your lender.

*As of November 25, 2026. Limits are from FHFA's November 24, 2026 announcement and its 2027 county list. They apply to loans eligible for sale to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac starting January 1, 2027.*

## What did FHFA announce?

The Federal Housing Finance Agency announced that "in most of the United States, the 2027 CLL for one-unit properties will be $855,400, an increase of $22,650 from 2026." In high-cost areas, the limit tops out at $1,283,100, "which is 150 percent of $855,400" ([FHFA, November 24, 2026](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/fhfa-announces-conforming-loan-limit-for-2027)).

The increase follows a formula. FHFA's expanded-data House Price Index showed prices rose 2.72% on average between the third quarters of 2025 and 2026, and the baseline limit rises by the same percentage. That's a smaller increase than last year, when the limit rose about 3.26% ([FHFA, November 25, 2025](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/fhfa-announces-conforming-loan-limit-values-for-2026)). FHFA said the limit will be higher in all but 24 U.S. counties.

A quick reminder of what this number is: the conforming loan limit is the largest loan Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can buy. Anything above your county's limit is a jumbo loan, which usually comes with its own, often stricter, requirements. I walked through the basics, and the lender estimates, in [my October preview of the 2027 limits](/insights/2027-conforming-loan-limits-california-buyers).

## How did the official number compare with what lenders guessed?

It beat all of them. In September, Rocket Mortgage and CrossCountry Mortgage began using an estimated 2027 limit of $845,000, United Wholesale Mortgage used $847,440 and Pennymac used $850,000 ([HousingWire, November 24, 2026](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fhfa-raises-2027-conforming-loan-limit-855400/)). The official $855,400 is $5,400 to $10,400 higher than those estimates, so the lenders that moved early didn't get caught out, and borrowers get a little more room than expected.

## What are the 2027 limits in my California county?

Here are the 2027 one-unit limits from FHFA's county list, with the 2026 limits for comparison where I have them from FHFA's 2026 list ([FHFA 2027 county list](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/d/cll/fullcountyloanlimitlist2027_hera-based_final_flat.csv); [FHFA 2026 county list](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/d/cll/fullcountyloanlimitlist2026_hera-based_final_flat.pdf)):

- **$1,283,100 (the ceiling, up from $1,249,125):** Los Angeles, Orange, San Francisco, San Mateo, Alameda, Santa Clara, Contra Costa, Marin. Santa Cruz and San Benito are also at $1,283,100 for 2027.
- **San Diego:** $1,143,100, up from $1,104,000.
- **Santa Barbara:** $1,063,750.
- **Ventura:** $1,043,050, up from $1,035,000.
- **San Luis Obispo:** $1,023,500.
- **Napa:** $1,017,750, unchanged from 2026.
- **Monterey:** $1,006,250.
- **Sonoma:** $897,000, unchanged from 2026.
- **$855,400 (the baseline, up from $832,750):** Riverside, San Bernardino, Sacramento, Placer, Fresno.

Napa and Sonoma were among the 24 counties nationally whose limits didn't rise, according to RealtyWire's review of FHFA's county data ([RealtyWire, November 24, 2026](https://realtywire.com/fhfa-2027-conforming-loan-limit/)). Limits can't go down, so those counties simply held.

## What about duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes?

Limits for two- to four-unit properties rise with the baseline too. For 2027, FHFA's county list shows:

- **Baseline counties** (Riverside, San Bernardino, Sacramento and others): $1,095,250 for two units, $1,323,900 for three and $1,645,350 for four.
- **Ceiling counties** (Los Angeles, Orange, the Bay Area counties above): $1,642,875 for two units, $1,985,850 for three and $2,468,025 for four.
- **San Diego:** $1,463,400 for two units, $1,768,900 for three and $2,198,300 for four.

If you're buying a small multifamily property, whether to live in one unit or as an investment, those jumps are bigger in dollar terms than the one-unit change, and they can matter just as much.

## Who actually benefits from the new limits?

Mostly borrowers whose loan amount falls in the gap between the 2026 and 2027 limits for their county. *Hypothetical examples, not offers:*

- A buyer in **Riverside County** who needs an $850,000 loan was over the 2026 limit of $832,750 and in jumbo territory. Under the 2027 limit of $855,400, that same loan amount fits as conforming.
- A buyer in **Los Angeles County** who needs a $1,270,000 loan was over the 2026 ceiling of $1,249,125. Under the 2027 ceiling of $1,283,100, it fits.
- An owner in **San Diego County** refinancing a $1,120,000 balance was over the 2026 limit of $1,104,000 and now falls under the 2027 limit of $1,143,100.

## I'm closing in December. Do I get the 2027 limit?

Not automatically. The 2027 limits take effect January 1, 2027 ([FHFA, Conforming Loan Limit FAQs, 2027](https://www.fhfa.gov/document/d/cll/fhfa-cll-faqs-2027.pdf)). What matters for Fannie Mae has been when the loan is delivered to it, not when it closes: for this year, Fannie Mae said the 2026 limits applied "to loans delivered to Fannie Mae in 2026 (even if originated prior to 1/1/2026)" ([Fannie Mae, Loan Limits](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/originating-underwriting/loan-limits)).

So if your loan falls between the 2026 and 2027 limits and you close in December, the question for your lender is whether it will close the loan now and hold it until January, which is what the lenders using early estimates have been doing. Some will. Others will price it as a jumbo or ask you to close in January. Get the answer in writing, along with how a January closing would affect your rate lock.

## What should I do if my loan is near the line?

1. **Look up your county's exact 2027 limit** on FHFA's list. Counties between the baseline and the ceiling, like San Diego, Ventura and Santa Barbara, each have their own number.
2. **Ask your lender which limit it's using today,** the 2026 limit, its own earlier estimate, or the official 2027 figure.
3. **Check your timing.** If you're early in the process, a January closing falls under the 2027 limits, but weigh that against rate and contract risk.
4. **Adjust the loan amount if you're just over.** A slightly bigger down payment, or a seller credit that frees up your cash, can bring a loan under the line. My [guide to seller credits this fall](/insights/california-buyers-leverage-seller-credits-fall-2026) covers how buyers are negotiating them.
5. **Compare a jumbo anyway.** For some borrowers with strong credit and reserves, a jumbo isn't the worse option.

## FAQ

**What is the 2027 conforming loan limit in California?** For a one-unit home it's $855,400 in baseline counties such as Riverside, San Bernardino, Sacramento and Fresno, and $1,283,100 in the highest-cost counties, including Los Angeles, Orange, San Francisco, San Mateo, Alameda and Santa Clara. Counties in between have their own limits: San Diego is $1,143,100 and Ventura is $1,043,050.

**When do the 2027 loan limits take effect?** January 1, 2027. FHFA announced them on November 24, 2026.

**Did my county's limit go up?** Most did. FHFA said limits rose in all but 24 U.S. counties. In California, Napa ($1,017,750) and Sonoma ($897,000) held at their 2026 levels.

**What are the 2027 limits for a duplex?** For two-unit properties, $1,095,250 in baseline counties and $1,642,875 in ceiling counties such as Los Angeles and Orange. San Diego's two-unit limit is $1,463,400.

**If I close in December, can I use the 2027 limit?** Only if your lender is willing to close and hold the loan until it can be delivered under the 2027 limits in January. Ask your lender directly and get the answer in writing.
