**The short answer:** Your mortgage rate is the guaranteed return on every extra dollar you pay toward it. With a 3% loan, paying extra earns 3%, and many families do better keeping that money liquid or invested. With a rate near 7%, extra payments become a strong, risk-free return. Either way, fund your emergency cushion and retirement match first; only then decide between the two.

## What does an extra $500 a month actually do?

The extra dollars are the same. The payoff is three times larger at the higher rate because each dollar of principal you retire stops costing you 6.75% instead of 3%.

## When paying extra makes sense

- Your rate is higher than what you'd reliably earn elsewhere after taxes.
- You're within 10 to 15 years of retirement and want lower fixed expenses.
- You already have six months of expenses saved and are maxing employer retirement matches.
- The peace of mind of a paid-off home matters to you. That's a legitimate reason.

## When keeping the cash usually wins

- Your rate is around 3% to 4% and safe savings pay close to or above it.
- Your income is variable: commission, self-employment, the trades, or a job facing AI-driven change.
- You may need a down payment soon for a move-up home, a rental or a child's first home.
- You have higher-rate debt, such as car loans or credit cards, to pay off first.

One point people overlook: **extra principal doesn't lower your monthly payment.** If income drops, money you put into the house helps only if you can borrow it back, and lenders approve HELOCs and refinances on current income.

## A middle path many of my clients use

1. **Build the cushion first:** six months of housing costs in savings.
2. **Capture the free money:** the full employer 401(k) or 403(b) match.
3. **Open a HELOC** while your income is strong, and leave it unused as backup.
4. **Split what's left:** some toward principal, some invested, based on your rate and how soon you'll need the money.
5. **Revisit when rates move.** If rates fall below yours, refinancing may beat any extra-payment plan.

## What about the mortgage interest deduction?

It can lower the effective cost of your mortgage if you itemize, but fewer households itemize than before. Ask your CPA what your after-tax rate really is. That's the number to compare against investments.

## FAQ

**Is it smart to pay off a 3% mortgage early?** Usually it's not urgent. Most households benefit more from cash reserves and retirement savings first.

**Does paying extra each month shorten my loan?** Yes, as long as the extra is applied to principal. Confirm with your servicer.

**Biweekly payments vs. one extra payment a year?** They reach roughly the same result. Avoid paid biweekly programs; you can do it yourself for free.

**Should I refinance instead?** If today's rate is meaningfully lower than yours and you'll stay long enough to recover closing costs, yes. Let's compare.
